## Station 1 — Where polymers come from (sources & polymerisation)

- Most polymers are made from **crude oil**, a **finite** (non-renewable) resource. Some are made from coal or natural gas.
- Journey: **crude oil is extracted** (drilled) → shipped/piped to a **refinery** → **fractional distillation** separates it into **fractions** by boiling point → heavy fractions are **cracked** (heat + catalyst) into smaller molecules such as **ethene** → these small molecules are **monomers** → **polymerisation** joins thousands of monomers into **long chains** = a **polymer**.
- Example: many ethene monomers → **poly(ethene)** (polythene). "Poly" = many; "mer" = unit.
- Polymers are usually supplied to manufacturers as **granules / pellets**.
- **Biopolymers / bioplastics** come from **plants** (renewable): e.g. **PLA (polylactic acid)** made from **corn starch or sugar cane**; it is **biodegradable** (compostable) and used for 3D-printing filament and food packaging.
- Key words: crude oil, finite, refinery, fractional distillation, fraction, cracking, monomer, polymerisation, polymer chain, biopolymer, PLA.

## Station 2 — Thermoforming vs thermosetting

- **Thermoforming polymers** (thermoplastics): long chains with **no cross-links**, only weak forces between chains. When **heated they soften** and can be **reshaped again and again**; they set hard when cooled. They can be **recycled** (remelted). Many have **plastic memory**: e.g. bent acrylic returns to flat when reheated.
- **Thermosetting polymers**: chains joined by strong **cross-links** in a rigid 3D network. They are set **once** (by heat/chemical reaction); **reheating does not soften them**, they **char/burn** instead. They **cannot be remoulded or easily recycled**. Usually more **heat resistant** and **rigid**.
- AQA thermoforming list: **Acrylic (PMMA), High impact polystyrene (HIPS), High density polythene (HDPE), Polypropylene (PP), Polyvinyl chloride (PVC), Polyethylene terephthalate (PET)**.
- AQA thermosetting list: **Epoxy resin (ER), Melamine formaldehyde (MF), Phenol formaldehyde (PF), Polyester resin (PR), Urea formaldehyde (UF)**.
- Memory hook: thermo**forming** = can be **formed** again; thermo**setting** = **set** for ever.

## Station 3 — Thermoforming polymers: properties & uses (the whodunit)

| Polymer | Key properties | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Acrylic (PMMA)** | stiff, hard, **glossy, can be polished**, transparent or coloured; **shatter-resistant (safer than glass)** but **scratches** easily and can be brittle | signs, display stands, shop windows, car rear lights, baths, school laser-cut projects |
| **HIPS** (high impact polystyrene) | rigid, lightweight, cheap, **good impact strength**, **easy to vacuum form** | vacuum-formed packaging trays, toys, model making, casings |
| **HDPE** (high density polythene) | hard, stiff, tough, **excellent chemical resistance**, food safe | milk & detergent bottles, buckets, bowls, crates, pipes, chopping boards |
| **PP** (polypropylene) | tough, flexible, lightweight, **good chemical & heat resistance**, food safe, **can make living hinges** (bends thousands of times) | flip-top bottle caps (living hinge), food containers, kitchen equipment, medical equipment, rope, garden chairs |
| **PVC** (polyvinyl chloride) | rigid and tough (flexible when plasticised), **weather resistant**, **good electrical insulator**, chemical resistant | drain pipes, guttering, window frames, **cable insulation** (flexible PVC), blister packs |
| **PET** (polyethylene terephthalate) | tough, lightweight, **clear**, good barrier to gas & moisture, **easily recycled** | **fizzy drinks & water bottles**, food packaging, fleece fibres |

## Station 4 — Thermosetting polymers: properties & uses

| Polymer | Key properties | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Epoxy resin (ER)** | **excellent electrical insulator**, hard, chemical resistant, **strong adhesive**; brittle unless reinforced | **two-part adhesives (e.g. Araldite)**, **printed circuit boards (PCBs)**, casting/encapsulating electronics, surface coatings, carbon-fibre composites |
| **Melamine formaldehyde (MF)** | hard, rigid, **scratch & stain resistant**, heat resistant, **food safe**, odourless | **picnic/children's tableware**, **laminated kitchen worktops**, electrical insulation |
| **Phenol formaldehyde (PF)** ("Bakelite") | **very heat resistant**, hard, brittle, good electrical insulator, usually dark coloured | **saucepan handles**, old electrical fittings, snooker balls |
| **Polyester resin (PR)** | rigid, hard, brittle on its own, heat & chemical resistant; **combined with glass fibre to make GRP** (glass-reinforced plastic) | **boat hulls**, car body panels, **casting/embedding** small objects |
| **Urea formaldehyde (UF)** | rigid, hard, strong, heat resistant, **good electrical insulator** | **plug sockets, light switches, electrical fittings**, handles & knobs, **adhesive in MDF** |

## Station 5 — Manufacturing processes

| Process | How it works (simple) | Polymer type / form | Products |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Injection moulding** | granules fed from a **hopper**, heated & pushed by a **screw**, **injected under pressure** into a closed mould, cooled, ejected | thermoforming granules | bottle caps, toy bricks, phone & remote casings, chairs |
| **Vacuum forming** | thermoforming **sheet heated** until soft, mould (former) raised, **air sucked out** so sheet is pulled over the mould. Moulds need **draft angles & rounded corners** | HIPS / acrylic sheet | packaging trays, blister packs, masks, chocolate box inserts |
| **Blow moulding** | a soft tube (**parison**) or injection-moulded **preform** is clamped in a mould and **air is blown in** to inflate it to the mould shape | HDPE, PET | **drinks bottles**, milk bottles, shampoo bottles |
| **Extrusion** | heated polymer pushed by a screw **through a shaped die**, continuously → long lengths with a **constant cross-section** | thermoforming granules | **pipes, guttering, window frames, rods, cable insulation** |
| **Line bending** | a **strip heater** softens a polymer sheet along a straight line, then it is bent (often with a jig) | acrylic / HIPS sheet | phone stands, display stands, brackets |
| **Rotational moulding** | polymer **powder** in a hollow mould that is **heated and rotated in two axes** | thermoforming powder | **large hollow products**: kayaks, traffic cones, water tanks, playground slides |
| **Compression moulding** | a measured amount of **thermosetting** powder/pellet is **pressed** in a heated mould and sets | thermosetting | **melamine plates**, UF plug sockets |
| **Laser cutting** | a laser beam cuts/engraves sheet material from a CAD drawing (CAM) | acrylic sheet | signs, keyrings, jewellery |
| **3D printing (FDM)** | filament melted and laid down layer by layer from a CAD model | PLA / ABS filament | prototypes, one-off parts |

## Station 6 — Stock forms, additives & finishes

- **Stock forms** (how polymers are bought): **sheet** (e.g. 3 mm acrylic), **film**, **granules/pellets** (for injection moulding & extrusion), **powder** (rotational & compression moulding), **foam**, **rod**, **tube**, **filament** (3D printing), extruded **profiles**.
- **Additives** change properties:
  - **Plasticisers** → make it **more flexible / softer** (rigid PVC → flexible PVC for cable insulation).
  - **Stabilisers (UV stabilisers)** → stop it breaking down, going **brittle or fading in sunlight** (garden furniture, window frames).
  - **Fillers** → **bulk it up and reduce cost** (e.g. chalk, wood flour).
  - **Flame retardants** → make it **less flammable** (electrical casings, furniture foam).
  - **Pigments / colourants** → give **colour all the way through**.
  - **Antistatic agents** → reduce **static and dust** sticking (electronics packaging).
  - **Biodegradable additives** → help it break down faster.
- **Finishes**: most polymers are **self-finishing** (colour runs all the way through; the mould gives the surface, gloss or texture) so no paint is needed. **Acrylic edges** are finished: **file → wet & dry paper → polish** (buffing wheel with polishing compound, or a polish such as Brasso). Graphics can be added by **printing, screen printing or vinyl decals**.

## Station 7 — Sustainability & recycling

- Crude oil is **finite**; extracting and processing it uses energy and causes pollution.
- Most oil-based polymers **are not biodegradable**; they can last **hundreds of years** in **landfill** or the **ocean** (microplastics harm wildlife).
- **Resin identification codes** (number in the recycling triangle):
  **1 PET** (drinks bottles) · **2 HDPE** (milk bottles) · **3 PVC** (pipes, window frames) · **4 LDPE** (carrier bags, squeezy bottles, cling film) · **5 PP** (yoghurt pots, bottle caps) · **6 PS** (polystyrene: foam cups, packaging) · **7 Other** (mixed / other polymers, e.g. PLA, acrylic).
- **Only thermoforming polymers can be recycled by remelting.** Thermosets cannot be remelted (they can be ground up and used as fillers).
- Recycling process: **collect → sort (by code) → wash → shred into flakes → melt → new granules → new products** (e.g. PET bottles → fleece jackets).
- Other options: **incineration with energy recovery** (burning to make electricity, but releases CO₂), **reuse**, **reduce** use.
- **Biopolymers** (e.g. **PLA from corn starch**, **Potatopak** from potato starch) are made from renewable plants and are **biodegradable / compostable**.
- **Oxo-degradable** polymers contain additives that make them break down faster in sunlight/oxygen.
